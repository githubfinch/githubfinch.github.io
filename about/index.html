What have I been doing?

Before heading off to undergrad, I was fully prepared to be studying illustration or fine art, But I have also been incredibly absorbed by the natural sciences since I was young, so after preparing for both paths at the same time, I finally decided that I will pursue the science path, and went to study physics at the University of Manchester.

Early on in my undergraduate years in Manchester, I read Frank Herbert's God Emperor of Dune, and marveled at the prospect of being able to predict how a system will behave based on sufficient knowledge of present and historical data. That system can be anything, a flock of birds, a group of voters, or a bunch of cells. On top of that, I also wanted to understand how a system of identical individuals following a few simple rules is capable of generating complex patterns. Following that line of thought, I took courses like statistical mechanics and non-linear dynamics, then enrolled in the course complex systems modelling at Kings College London.

During my masters degree I gained experience in training neural networks in recognising handwritten numbers in the MNIST database, co-curated the exhibition "It All Adds Up: Global Discoveries in Maths", and learnt and applied C for my master's thesis (Checkout my Github page for the codes for my projects and the sourcecode for this website!).

Since my graduation in 2025, I got married, saw an Eclipse in Madrid, moved to Germany and joined IDCN in Munich as an events team volunteer. I am eager to see how life pans out from here!
